AGROLATTICE 11.5 — Decision Intelligence & Research Optimisation
================================================================
Based on: AGROLATTICE 11.4 — Multimodal Crop Intelligence & Hybrid Twin Learning
Application version: 20.5-release11.5-decision-intelligence

PURPOSE
-------
Release 11.5 turns the modelling/evidence infrastructure into a researcher-facing decision
workflow. It deliberately separates prediction, recommendation, actual field operation and
measured outcome. The new tools compare alternatives, expose trade-offs, retain provenance
and support later observational effectiveness audits instead of presenting one opaque "AI
recommendation" as agronomic truth.

WHAT CHANGED
------------
1. Decision Intelligence & Research Optimisation workspace
   - Added to Crop Decisions and discoverable from Models & Evidence.
   - Seven researcher-oriented tabs: Decision home, Irrigation policy studio, Nutrient
     optimisation, State assimilation, Recommendation trials, Causal audit, and Methods &
     safeguards.
   - Readiness/status cards explain which evidence is available before analysis.
   - Compare -> choose -> record -> apply only if approved -> measure -> evaluate workflow.

2. Irrigation Policy Studio
   - Uses mapped fields and validated crop/soil profiles rather than requiring CSV setup.
   - Weather can come directly from NASA POWER for the mapped field, the Research Data Hub,
     or an uploaded future scenario/forecast table.
   - Automatically records NASA acquisition provenance in the Research Evidence database.
   - Reuses the existing FAO-style daily root-zone water balance and crop-stage parameters.
   - Compares rainfed, RAW-trigger, deficit-refill and fixed-interval strategies under the
     same weather/soil/crop assumptions.
   - Can add a recorded soil-moisture sensor-threshold strategy using existing Field
     Operations sensor readings. This remains simulation/decision support only.
   - Exposes application efficiency, maximum event, initial depletion, root depths, runoff,
     trigger/refill ranges, optional seasonal water-allocation/event-count constraints and economics.
   - Infeasible alternatives remain visible with constraint reasons instead of disappearing.
   - Computes water use, event count, stress/severe-stress days, ET satisfaction, deep
     percolation, optional Ky-based relative-yield proxy and optional irrigation-adjusted
     margin.
   - Displays Pareto-efficient alternatives and water-vs-crop-stress trade-offs before any
     objective-selected scenario is shown.
   - Saves the whole comparison as a decision run and can save a selected scenario as a
     research recommendation without silently writing it as an applied irrigation event.

3. Local N-P-K response and multi-objective optimisation
   - Can use the Research Data Hub or a researcher-supplied trial/field table.
   - Existing nutrient samples and fertiliser operations are shown as field context when a
     field is selected; product rate is not silently converted to elemental N/P/K.
   - Fits a second-order empirical N/P/K response surface with Ridge regularisation.
   - Supports additional numeric G×E×M covariates and group-aware validation by site,
     season, trial or another researcher-selected grouping variable.
   - Search ranges default to the observed support of the fitted data.
   - Generates a transparent candidate grid and Pareto screens predicted outcome against
     total nutrient use and optional margin.
   - Researcher objectives: balanced trade-off, maximum predicted yield, minimum nutrient
     rate retaining >=95% of predicted maximum, or maximum margin when economics are given.
   - Provides validation residuals, explicit extrapolation boundaries, export and persistent
     decision/recommendation records.

4. Uncertainty-weighted state assimilation
   - Adds single-observation Gaussian updates, independent time-varying model-state updates, and
     recursive repeated-measurement updates for a static/calibration state.
   - Evolving states such as LAI can use per-time-point model prior mean/SD and observation
     mean/SD; AGROLATTICE does not incorrectly carry a posterior across biological time.
   - Recursive sequential updating is explicitly restricted to repeated measurements of the same
     latent state/parameter unless a process/state-transition model has propagated the prior.
   - Displays posterior trajectory and persists state-assimilation evidence.
   - The UI explicitly requires model and observation to represent the same physical state
     and units, or to use a separately validated observation operator.
   - EO indices are not automatically treated as LAI, biomass, N status or soil water.

5. Recommendation trials and outcome tracking
   - Universal recommendation records can be created for a field/trial/experimental unit.
   - Status lifecycle supports Proposed, Accepted, Rejected, Applied, Completed and
     Superseded, with an append-only status-history event and optional researcher reason for
     every transition.
   - Researchers can record whether a recommendation was followed, the actual action,
     action date, measured outcome, unit and pre-treatment/context covariates.
   - Recommendations and treatment outcomes remain separate records.
   - Creating a Field Operations task from a research recommendation requires explicit user
     acknowledgement; no recommendation is automatically treated as an operation.

6. Observational causal-effect audit
   - Can analyse recommendation/outcome records directly from the registry, a Data Hub table
     or an uploaded study table.
   - Accepts numeric or categorical binary treatment labels (e.g. Yes/No, A/B, True/False) and requires an explicit treated/intervention level so effect direction is never inferred alphabetically.
   - Provides naive difference as a diagnostic only, inverse-probability weighting, separate
     outcome-model/T-learner estimates and a doubly robust AIPW-style estimate.
   - Pre-treatment adjustment can include numeric and categorical factors such as genotype,
     site or management class; categorical levels are reference-coded and exposed in balance diagnostics.
   - Nuisance models are cross-fitted; researcher can select a field/site/season/trial group
     for grouped cross-fitting and clustered bootstrap.
   - Reports propensity overlap, positivity warnings, IPW effective sample size, raw and
     weighted standardised mean differences, unit-level diagnostics and a treatment-shuffle
     placebo/refutation diagnostic.
   - Confidence intervals use a fast non-parametric bootstrap of the cross-fitted effect
     scores (cluster bootstrap when a group is supplied); nuisance models are not refit in
     every bootstrap replicate and the app states this limitation.
   - Researchers can save an adjustment rationale / causal-graph note explaining why selected
     covariates are plausible pre-treatment common causes and why post-treatment variables are excluded.
   - Results are explicitly conditional on consistency, exchangeability given the selected
     pre-treatment covariates, positivity, no interference and model/measurement assumptions.
   - Causal audits are saved with assumptions and diagnostics, never promoted as causal proof.

7. Research Evidence database schema 1.3.0
   - Additive new tables: decision_runs, state_assimilations, causal_analyses and
     recommendation_status_history; state_assimilations also stores optional sequence_json.
   - Existing datasets, acquisitions, models, predictions, recommendations and treatment
     outcomes remain intact.
   - Automatic pre-upgrade SQLite backup, migration, integrity and foreign-key verification.
   - Field Operations, Maize Pollination Lab and Persistent Twin database schemas are unchanged.

8. Research Evidence UI
   - Registry summary now includes treatment outcomes, decision runs and causal audits.
   - New Decision evidence tab exposes decision runs, state assimilations and causal analyses.

9. Release/startup/migration integration
   - RUN_APP.bat preflights the new decision modules and Research Evidence schema 1.3.0.
   - Backup-first migration scripts now identify Release 11.5 and retain source-authoritative
     database migration with rollback/integrity/row-count safeguards.
   - Current user guide and Help workspace point to Release 11.5 documentation.

SCIENTIFIC ADAPTATIONS, NOT CLAIMED REPRODUCTIONS
-------------------------------------------------
- Irrigation decision structure is inspired by the reviewed Seq2Seq + deep-RL irrigation
  literature, but Release 11.5 does NOT claim the exact published Seq2Seq or DRL policy.
  It uses transparent root-zone policy comparison as the safer researcher-facing baseline.
- Multi-objective nutrient optimisation adapts the Pareto concept from spatial fertiliser
  optimisation research. Release 11.5 uses a transparent empirical response surface and
  exhaustive candidate grid, NOT the paper's exact NSGA-II workflow.
- State assimilation provides a general uncertainty-weighted same-state update foundation;
  it is NOT an exact reproduction of crop-specific EO/crop-model N assimilation.
- Causal recommendation auditing independently adapts standard backdoor-adjusted observational
  estimators motivated by Tsoumas et al. It does not reproduce a proprietary pipeline and does
  not replace randomised or well-designed field experiments.

DEPENDENCIES
------------
No new mandatory third-party package was added for Release 11.5. The decision engine uses the
existing NumPy, pandas, scikit-learn, Plotly and AGROLATTICE root-zone/crop libraries. Optional
research-model dependencies from 11.4 remain optional.

KNOWN LIMITATIONS
-----------------
- NASA POWER is gridded historical/near-real-time environmental data, not a local station and
  not a future operational forecast.
- Irrigation policy outputs do not control pumps, valves or external controllers.
- Ky-based relative yield in irrigation comparison is a water-stress proxy, not a calibrated
  field yield model unless independently calibrated.
- Empirical nutrient optimisation requires measured outcomes and real N/P/K rate variation.
  Its recommendation validity is limited by the fitted data and validation design.
- The state-assimilation tool assumes compatible states/units and independent Gaussian errors.
- Observational causal estimates can remain biased by unmeasured confounding, poor overlap,
  interference, measurement error or misspecified adjustment sets.
- Pareto membership does not mean an alternative is agronomically safe or operationally valid.
- Research recommendations require researcher review and remain distinct from applied operations.
