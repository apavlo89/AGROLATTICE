AGROLATTICE 11.8 - scientific basis for the Mechanistic Maize Twin (model unchanged from 11.0)
==============================================================================

Primary mechanistic source
--------------------------
Laurent A. et al. (2025). Predicting inbred parent synchrony at flowering for
maize hybrid seed production by integrating crop growth model with whole genome
prediction. Crop Science 65, e21453. DOI: 10.1002/csc2.21453.

AGROLATTICE implements the equations disclosed in this paper's Maize Flowering
Synchrony (MFS) crop-growth model:

* 30.6 GDD from planting to emergence.
* Leaf number = 2.5 * exp(post-emergence GDD * coblf), capped at tln.
* Ear-growth onset at Vn = 0.67 * tln.
* Ear biomass starts at 0.01 g and grows exponentially toward 5 g.
* Female 50% silking occurs when predicted ear biomass reaches ebR1.
* Male anthesis occurs 40 GDD after final-leaf expansion.

The genotype-specific physiological parameters are total leaf number (tln),
coefficient of leaf appearance (coblf), and ear biomass at 50% silking (ebR1).
Publication priors used as defaults are tln N(19,2), coblf N(0.0019,0.00036),
and ebR1 N(2,0.5). They are priors, not measurements for a local parent line.

What is not reproduced
----------------------
The paper's commercial phenotype/genotype data and original C++ Bayesian
CGM-WGP sampler are not public. AGROLATTICE does not claim to reproduce that
proprietary layer. The optional SNP-marker feature is a transparent genomic
ridge approximation that predicts calibrated physiological parameters. Its
outputs are explicitly labelled as an approximation.

Calibration and uncertainty
---------------------------
AGROLATTICE fits parent physiology with prior-regularised nonlinear least
squares using observed flowering dates and repeated tagged-plant leaf counts.
The priors are necessary because different parameter combinations can produce
similar flowering dates. Uncertainty is propagated by Monte Carlo sampling of
tln, coblf and ebR1. It does not cover every weather-forecast, water-stress,
disease, pollen-quantity or operational source of uncertainty.

Operational sowing strategy
----------------------------
The optimiser can compare one male sowing date with two staggered dates. It
targets male shed timing approximately two days before and two days after
female silking, following the operational example in Laurent et al. It predicts
timing rather than pollen quantity and must be field-validated.

Additional sources
------------------
Noriega Gonzalez L.A. et al. (2011). Fenologia, crecimiento y sincronia floral
de los progenitores del hibrido de maiz QPM H-374C. Revista Mexicana de Ciencias
Agricolas 2(4), 489-500. The parent-specific 30-GDD difference reported for its
Celaya experiment is not treated as a universal offset.

Field observations remain primary evidence. All recommendations require
